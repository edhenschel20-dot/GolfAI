# Charging Station Enclosure — Design Concepts, Round 1

Author: room-designer agent
Source of truth: `/home/user/GolfAI/CLAUDE.md`
Status: awaiting project-reviewer evaluation

Room context driving all three concepts: medium-toned oak credenza, ornate
brass drawer pulls, traditional (not minimalist) styling, a large leaning
family-photo canvas, two live plants, a woven basket. The enclosure must read
as *furniture*, not gadget housing. All three concepts share one design
language — raised-panel/fluted faces, warm oak tone, real brass hardware —
so any of them could be flanked by a matching second unit later (Expansion
requirement) without redesign.

Shared assumptions (flagged, not invented requirements):
- Credenza top depth is a typical 16–20 in; all footprints below stay within
  ~8 in of depth to leave walking room in front of the canvas and plants.
- Canvas is a standard 16x20 to 24x36 leaning canvas. Concept 1 needs the
  actual width measured before geometry work.
- Brick envelope 6.77 x 4.01 x 1.41 in is the fixed internal core; every
  concept keeps a rear-facing vent path and a rear AC-cord exit, and none of
  them bury the Echo Dot.

---

## Ranking

1. **Concept 1 — "Gallery Mantel"** (canvas plinth). Best answer to the
   brief's own hint; boldest transformation of the clutter zone into decor.
   *Tradeoff: largest footprint and a guaranteed multi-part print; needs the
   canvas measured first.*
2. **Concept 2 — "Keepsake Chest"** (faux apothecary/jewelry chest). Most
   convincing "this came with the credenza" disguise per square inch;
   easiest to print and finish.
   *Tradeoff: most understated — it sits beside the canvas rather than
   fixing the whole tableau, and the top deck gets busy with 4 stations.*
3. **Concept 3 — "Conservatory Bench"** (planter bench). Best use of the
   existing greenery as camouflage; most charming.
   *Tradeoff: water and electronics share one object — needs a sealed
   liner and a hard internal partition, which the reviewer may kill on
   principle.*

My recommendation: pursue **Concept 1 as primary** with **Concept 2 as the
low-risk fallback**. Concept 3 is worth keeping alive only if the reviewer
accepts the liner/partition mitigation.

## Concept 1 — "Gallery Mantel" (canvas base / plinth) — DISGUISE CONCEPT

**What it pretends to be:** a low mantel-style display plinth that the family
photo canvas stands on. The canvas stops leaning awkwardly behind clutter and
becomes the intentional centerpiece; the "charging station" visually
disappears because the whole object reads as a picture stand.

**Form.** A long, low rectangular plinth, roughly **24 in W x 7.5 in D x
4.5 in H** (width to be tuned to canvas width + ~2 in reveal each side).
Traditional profile: a stepped crown lip around the top edge (like a mantel
shelf), a recessed raised-panel front face echoing the credenza's drawer
fronts, and a small ogee base molding so it doesn't look like a slab. The top
deck has a shallow full-width groove near the back edge with a low rear rail:
the canvas drops into the groove and leans a few degrees back against the
rail — held, not clamped. The plinth is centered on the credenza; plants and
basket flank it.

**Device placement (concept level).**
- *Brick:* lies flat inside the plinth belly, centered — 4.5 in external
  height leaves ~3+ in internal clearance over a 1.41 in brick, generous for
  cable slack and airflow. Rear face carries a vent band disguised as a run
  of decorative dentil molding (real through-slots between "teeth"); AC cord
  exits low at rear center.
- *Watch pucks:* the flagship feature. Left of the canvas on the top deck,
  two shallow circular wells side by side ("His"/"Hers" embossed beneath),
  each with a brass-ringed collar; puck sits flush in the well, cable drops
  through a hidden channel into the belly. Watches lie flat, glanceable,
  jewelry-tray style.
- *Phone rotator:* right of the canvas. The rotator body sinks into a
  recessed bay in the deck so only the cradle arm rises above the surface;
  from across the room it reads like a small easel or phone stand on a shelf.
  Bay is open-topped so the arm's rocking sweep is unobstructed.
- *Echo Dot:* right end of the plinth terminates in a short open-topped
  round turret (a "bookend" post) the Dot nests into, sitting proud by
  ~1 in: top mic array fully open to the room, side speaker ring above deck
  level, turret wall pierced with a vertical-slat grille. Not buried.
- *Swappable ports:* three labeled cable ports emerge through brass-ringed
  grommets on the front-right deck edge, each with an embossed nameplate.

**Materials/finish.** Print in wood-filled PLA (e.g., Hatchbox Wood or
eSUN wood PLA), sand to 220, then **gel-stain (golden-oak family) + matte
polyurethane** — gel stain sits on the surface like paint but drags like
stain, and on sanded wood-fill it convincingly reads as oak from 3 ft away.
Brass accents are *real hardware*, not printed: stock brass grommet rings on
the puck wells and cable ports, and two small brass rosettes or knobs on the
front raised panels to echo the drawer pulls. Layer lines oriented as
horizontal "grain."

**Footprint/height vs. credenza.** ~24 x 7.5 in footprint on a credenza top;
4.5 in tall plus canvas. Raises the canvas 4.5 in — sightline check needed
if art hangs above.

**Expansion path.** A second matching plinth of the same profile can butt
against the first end-to-end (the crown/base moldings are designed to
continue across the joint), or a shorter matching "wing" module flanks it.

**Honest risks.** Exceeds any common print bed — 3 to 4 body segments with
hidden joints under the moldings (printing-expert problem, but flagged now).
Canvas dimensions must be measured before any geometry.

## Concept 2 — "Keepsake Chest" (faux jewelry/apothecary chest)

**What it pretends to be:** a small traditional two-drawer keepsake chest or
jewelry box with a valet top — the kind of object that already lives on
credenzas in rooms like this. Sits beside the canvas, not under it.

**Form.** Upright chest, roughly **11 in W x 7.5 in D x 7 in H**. Front face
is two faux drawer fronts (non-functional, printed as raised-panel reliefs)
each wearing a **real ornate brass pull matched to the credenza's** — this
one detail does 80% of the disguise. Slightly overhanging top with a rounded
edge, plinth base with bracket feet. The entire top is the working valet
deck; the body below is the cable/brick vault, accessed by a lift-off back
panel (invisible from the room).

**Device placement.**
- *Brick:* stands on edge or lies flat in the lower vault; rear panel is a
  full-height slatted vent (reads as the "unfinished back" every real chest
  has — nobody styles the back of casegoods). AC cord exits low rear.
- *Watch pucks:* front half of the valet top: two flush puck wells in a
  shallow brass-railed tray area (a miniature gallery rail on three sides,
  stock brass rod + printed posts), "His"/"Hers" embossed. The rail makes it
  read as a valet tray and keeps watches from sliding off.
- *Echo Dot:* rear-left corner of the top, in a half-recessed round cradle
  (Dot sits ~40% proud): open top, open sides above deck line. It reads as a
  round trinket sitting on the chest.
- *Phone rotator:* rear-right of the top; body recessed into a bay so only
  the cradle arm shows, with clearance for its rocking sweep. The phone
  leans like a small standing frame when docked.
- *Swappable ports:* three labeled brass-grommet ports on the right side
  face near the front edge — reachable without seeing the back.

**Materials/finish.** Same system as Concept 1 (wood-fill PLA + gel stain +
matte poly) for family consistency. Alternate for a richer look: matte PLA
painted in a two-step faux-grain (golden oak base coat, burnt-umber glaze
dragged with a cheap graining comb), which out-performs raw wood filament on
ornate raised-panel detail. Brass: real pulls + Rub 'n Buff gold on the
printed rail posts and well collars.

**Footprint/height.** Smallest footprint of the three (~11 x 7.5 in); at
7 in tall it sits comfortably below the canvas's lower third and beside it.

**Expansion path.** Literally buy-another-drawer-pull and print a mirrored
twin; two chests flanking the canvas would look *more* intentional, not
less — the strongest expansion story of the three.

**Honest risks.** Top deck density: pucks + Dot + rotator on ~11 x 7.5 in is
tight; the rotator's sweep envelope must be confirmed before committing. If
it doesn't fit, the rotator moves to a small matching side pedestal —
acceptable but weakens the "one tidy object" story.

## Concept 3 — "Conservatory Bench" (planter bench) — DISGUISE CONCEPT #2

**What it pretends to be:** a low plant bench/riser with the existing pothos
sitting on one end, its vines trailing over the charging side — the
greenery itself becomes the cable camouflage.

**Form.** A low bench, roughly **16 in W x 7.5 in D x 5 in H** at the deck,
with the left third stepped up ~1.5 in as a **plant pedestal**: a round
recess sized to the existing pothos pot (pot sits *in* a printed cachepot
socket with a sealed drip liner — the plant is on the bench, never over the
electronics). Turned-look bracket legs and an arched front apron give it a
traditional footstool silhouette. The right two-thirds deck is the charging
valet; the hollow bench body below is the brick vault.

**Device placement.**
- *Brick:* in the right-side hollow, separated from the planter socket by a
  **solid double-wall partition** — the planter recess is a printed cup
  with no openings into the electronics bay; any overwater ends up on the
  credenza, not in the wiring. Vents: the arched apron cutouts front and
  back are the airflow path (decorative and functional). AC cord exits rear
  under the pedestal step.
- *Watch pucks:* two brass-collared flush wells on the main deck, forward
  edge, His/Hers embossed.
- *Echo Dot:* nests in an open ring seat at the deck's rear-right corner,
  half-proud, sides and top open; trailing vines are trained *behind* it,
  never over the mic.
- *Phone rotator:* body recessed at deck center-rear, cradle arm exposed,
  sweep clearance kept clear of the vine zone.
- *Swappable ports:* three labeled grommets along the rear deck edge.

**Materials/finish.** Wood-fill PLA + gel stain + matte poly like the
others; the planter cup interior gets a brushed-on waterproof coating
(or simply holds a cheap nursery-pot saucer as the true liner). Brass:
collar rings + a small brass nameplate on the apron.

**Footprint/height.** ~16 x 7.5 in; 5 in deck, 6.5 in at pedestal. Sits to
one side of the canvas, consolidating plant + chargers into one vignette and
actually *freeing* credenza surface.

**Expansion path.** Mirror-image second bench (pedestal on the right) flanks
the canvas symmetrically — a genuinely nice end-state.

**Honest risks.** Water near electronics is a reviewer red flag; the
mitigation (sealed socket, hard partition, external drip path) is real but
adds parts and assembly. Vine training is a living-system dependency — the
disguise improves over months, it isn't perfect on day one.

## Notes for downstream agents

- **Electronics-expert:** none of these forms constrains your port
  assignment; all three give you a rear vent band, a rear AC exit, hidden
  puck-cable channels, and 3 labeled swappable grommets. Concept 1 has the
  most interior volume; Concept 2 the least — check bend radii there.
- **Project-reviewer:** the decision I need from you is (a) Concept 1 vs 2
  as primary, and (b) whether Concept 3's water mitigation is acceptable or
  disqualifying. Also please get the canvas width/height measured — it
  gates Concept 1 entirely.
- **Printing-expert:** all concepts assume the wood-fill-PLA + gel-stain +
  real-brass-hardware finish system; if wood-fill is a problem (nozzle wear,
  detail loss on moldings), the faux-grain paint route in Concept 2 is the
  approved fallback for the whole family.
